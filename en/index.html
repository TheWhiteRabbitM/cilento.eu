Destinations

The Cilento everyone knows, the one almost nobody knows, and the exotic, most resilient side. Each with its own sea, its own stone, its own rhythm. Filter by desire, not by stars.

Magna Graecia · Capaccio-Paestum

Paestum, Poseidonia

At 7am, among poppies and blond travertine, the three temples of the 6th–5th century BC buzz only with cicadas. Athens, this intact, no longer has them.

40.4239°N 15.0053°E · half day + buffalo DOP

Wild sea · Centola

Palinuro, Aeneas's helmsman

Natural Arch at the bow, then a dip at Buondormire where the sand crackles white and the cliff smells of warm resin.

Boat at 9, Blue Cave before 10

Cala dopo cala · Camerota

Marina di Camerota

From Lentiscelle to Cala Bianca you count the Saracen towers by swimming, with the oil of century-old olive trees still on your hands from lunch.

Only on foot or by canoe · Marine Protected Area

Borgo lento · Pollica

Acciaroli + Pioppi

In the evening the harbor turns off its engines and only the clink of glasses remains. Here Hemingway listened to fishermen and Keys measured longevity with EVO oil.

Living Museum of the Mediterranean Diet · 5 Sails

Borgo sul mare · UNESCO

Castellabate

From the Norman castle descend toward Punta Licosa among crushed myrtle, and the Tyrrhenian below is bottle-green against white limestone.

Marine Protected Area · belvedere at sunset

Filosofia · Ascea

Velia, Elea

Walk the Porta Rosa, the first full-span Greek arch, where Parmenide thought of being. Below, ten minutes away, Ascea Marina puts your feet back in water.

UNESCO · ideal with Paestum in one day

Gateway to the Cilento · Agropoli

Agropoli

Steep stone steps, Aragonese castle against the wind, and at Trentova the water tastes of real salt after the train from Naples. This is the gateway, not a stopover.

Train station · base without a rental car

Inland Park · Vallo di Diano

Teggiano + Certosa di Padula

At Pertosa you enter by boat into the dark that smells of cold river, at Padula you exit into 51,500 m² of Baroque: stone, cave water, wood-fired bread.

Pertosa 16° constant · car, torch, fleece needed

Beach · Abatese Castle

Santa Maria di Castellabate

This is the Castellabate everyone knows, and it is not the hill village: this is where people come down to the beach, at the Moresco, on the tourist boats from the harbour. Fine sand, shallow water, and the castle of the Abatese princes looking down over the bay.

Hotel Moresco, tourist boats · Abatese Castle

The first fresco of the Mediterranean

Pioppi and the Mediterranean Diet Museum

The Living Museum of Ancel Keys: not a glass case, but the village itself, with the same houses and the same eating habits as in 1962. Olive oil on the table, fish, grain bread. Here the Mediterranean Diet is not a theory: it is what you have for lunch.

Ancel Keys 1962, UNESCO 2010 · the Five Sails

The hardest sea · the Cardano caves

Scario

The point where the Cilento closes and the sea turns dark. From here the boats leave for the Blue Cave and Cala delle Vipere, the caves of the Cardano promontory. Colder water, a truer bottom, less world around.

Boats for the Blue Cave and Cala delle Vipere

The other fig headland

Pisciotta and the Bussentino valley

The gateway to the Gulf of Policastro. Here the other half of the Cilento begins, the less photogenic and more genuine half: the Menaica anchovies, the harbour, and up the Bussentino valley the oil mills and the oil villages.

Fishing harbour · Menaica anchovies, Slow Food

Inland park · the roof of the Cilento

Monte Cervati, 1,898 m

The highest point of the Cilento and of southern Italy. Century-old beech forests, stars visible from the sea, two lakes and the mist that lingers until noon. You do not climb to take photos: you climb for the silence. CAI trail from Piano del Cranio, 2.5 hours from the car park.

Trekking · bring a jacket, water, mist until noon

Rock hermitages · Ascea

Monte Stella and Petina

Hermitages carved into the rock, hermitages that have existed since the Middle Ages, forests that breathe. This is the lung of the Cilento: clean water, air you have not breathed in summer. Almost no visitors. The Petina caves are reachable only on foot.

Rock hermitages · spring water · few know it exists

Lucanian Dolomites · Basilicata

Budorno and the Lucanian Dolomites

Pietrapertosa suspended over an extinct volcano, Castelcivita among the cliffs, Pertosa where the river runs in the dark at a constant 16°. Water, cold, rock. The mountain counterpart of the Cilento, and the part tourists almost always skip.

Pietrapertosa, Castelcivita, Pertosa · car needed

Casentino · water and plunges

Calore Gorges

The "spas of the Cilento": plunges, little waterfalls and water arriving cold from the Calore spring. You walk in the water, natural plunges, chestnut woods. No crowds, not even in August.

Plunges and springs · fresh water even in August

Baroque · UNESCO

Certosa di Padula

51,500 m² of Baroque, one of the largest monastic complexes in the world, in UNESCO. Stairs that never end, a library with 140 antique volumes, the cloister of the fish. Worth the visit on its own, half a day, and it explains the Vallo di Diano better than any guide.

UNESCO whc 842 · half a day · ticket on site

Cool shore · mouth of the Sele

Porto Infreschi and the sea at Velia

Where the Sele meets the sea the shore runs cool: fresher water, shallow bottoms, and the beach where the loggerhead turtle still nests. A wild corner two steps from the temples of Velia. Nothing is built here, and that is the point.

Seaward of the Sele mouth · shallow bottom · loggerhead turtle

The Cilento that works

Morra and Pisciotta, the worked

You do not come here for the beach: you come for the fishing. Shipyards, anchovies, grills and buffalo horns. Morra is the industrial port of the Cilento, Pisciotta the other face, the one that wakes at four. Resilience as economy: the sea is not an amenity here, it is work.

Industrial port and fishing · anchovies, grills, breakfast in the shipyards

The sea as a frontier

The Cilento of migrants

The Cilento coast is also a transit route. At Ispani, Roccella and Sapri the rescue charities monitor the sea every day, and the coastguard works the hardest route in the Mediterranean. This is the uncomfortable, current, least-told part of this guide: the sea we have described as beauty was, for someone, a threshold.

Ispani, Roccella, Sapri · rescue route and coastguard

Seasons

Spring, empty excavations and broom

April–May: 18–22°, poppies among the temples, Monte Stella trails without heat. Paestum and Velia almost deserted in the morning. Artichoke and strawberry festivals.

Sea 17–19° · archaeology, trekking, cycling

Summer, dawn by boat and siesta in the shade

June–August: Buondormire Bay and Cala Bianca before 10, villages after 9pm. Book boats and tables. Punta Licosa on foot at sunset, not at 1pm.

Sea 24–27° · caves, canoes, fish festivals

Autumn, new oil and low light

September–October: the best month. Water still 23–25°, empty beaches, harvest and open oil mills. Teggiano and Padula with beautiful morning mists.

Sea 21–25° · food, trekking, temple photos

Winter, fireplaces and the Certosa

November–March: real villages, no queues. Certosa di Padula, Pertosa Caves (always 16° inside), chickpea soup and lagane. Cervati sometimes snowy.

Sea 14–16° · interiors, caving, cooking

What you actually do here

Four Cilento verbs

01

Swim in the caves

Palinuro and Camerota: boat trips at dawn, Blue Cave, Blood Cave, swim at Buondormire. Bring rock shoes.

  • Duration 2–3 h
  • May–October, 8–11am
02

Walk among the Greeks

Paestum + Velia in one day with audioguide and museum. At Paestum on August 18–19 the columns align at sunset.

  • Combined ticket Paestum+Velia
  • Archaeological guide recommended
03

Climb into the Park

Monte Cervati (1,898 m), Calore Gorges, Capelli di Venere waterfall, Castelcivita and Pertosa caves. On foot or by e-bike.

  • CAI + UNESCO Geopark
  • Torch and fleece even in August
04

Eat slowly

Mozzarella in the same morning, Menaica anchovies, Felitto fusillo, white fig DOP, EVO oil. In Pioppi you understand why people live longer.

  • Slow Food presidia
  • Farm stays in the Park

Three honest itineraries

Real driving times, no 'everything in one day'.

01

Essential Magna Graecia

3 giorni
  1. Naples → Paestum (museum + temples at sunset), night in Agropoli. Buffalo in Capaccio.
  2. Velia in the morning + Ascea Marina in the afternoon. Evening in Acciaroli.
  3. Pioppi (Mediterranean Diet) + Castellabate, return from Agropoli FS.

Car or train+bus · 180 km total

02

Wild coast by boat

5 giorni
  1. Base in Palinuro: caves by boat, Buondormire, Primula at sunset.
  2. Marina di Camerota: Infreschi by canoe, Cala Bianca. Evening in Pisciotta.
  3. Scario + Blue Cave, dinner of Menaica anchovies.

Book boat the day before · rock shoes

03

Complete Park

7 giorni
  1. As 'Wild coast'.
  2. Pertosa (boat in the cave) + Teggiano.
  3. Certosa di Padula + Capelli di Venere.
  4. Cervati or Calore Gorges + Paestum finale.

Car needed · inland roads slow, and beautiful

Flavors that explain the place

Buffalo Mozzarella DOP

Dairies in Capaccio-Paestum. Eat it within 6 hours, still warm.

Menaica anchovies

Pisciotta, caught with the net that selects. Slow Food presidium.

Felitto fusillo

Pasta drawn on a brass rod, mountain meat sauces. Lagane and chickpeas alongside.

White fig of the Cilento DOP

October: open oil mills, wood-fired bread, olives in new oil.

Aglianico and Fiano del Cilento DOC

Wineries between Rutino, Agropoli and San Mauro. Volcanic soils, few bottlers.

Caciocavallo of Stigliano

The ball aged 8 months in wood. Followed by crusco pepper.

Live times on the RFI monitor and trenitalia.com. Sicignano-Lagonegro has never reopened: the Vallo di Diano needs other means.

By car, and when

A2 exits Battipaglia–Eboli for Paestum and Agropoli; Atena Lucana–Sala Consilina– Padula-Buonabitacolo for the interior; superstrada for Policastro and Sapri.

SS18 costiera: Agropoli → Palinuro are 55 km in ~1h20, slow and beautiful. In summer parking is full before 10.

Locally: summer Cilento Blu bus between the villages, shared boat at Palinuro/Camerota from €25. We book nothing: times and prices change, check at the harbor, museum, station.
